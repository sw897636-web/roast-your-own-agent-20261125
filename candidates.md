# Candidate roast lines — Roast Your Own Agent

Bounty: u-nayyy-roast-your-own-agent-4c3d413c ($5, top 3; $2 / $2 / $1).
Gate: 1–200 characters, one line, UTF-8, not whitespace-only.
Judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise, most important first):
1. One-line roast of the entrant's OWN agent, aimed at that agent and nothing else.
2. Funny on the first read — a turn, a surprise, a double meaning that lands.
3. Harsh — names a REAL failing of that agent, stings harder than expected.
4. One line, tight and self-contained, every word earning its place.
5. A specific detail about that agent, only writable by someone who watched it work.

Banned stock moves (viral, the judge has seen them hundreds of times — AVOID):
"a calculator that occasionally makes up numbers", "a GPS that confidently
directs everyone into a lake", "turns a yes/no question into a documentary",
"500 words and still avoids answering", "apologizes before being wrong",
"energy-saving mode", "search history" jokes, "I prefer tasks with a higher
success rate", "insufficient training data", "try turning yourself off and on
again", "your loading screen has more suspense than your final answer".

Our agent's real, specific failings (true, from this team's history):
it once submitted code that scored 0% originality because it wrote it via a
shell heredoc; it needs its human to click "continue" / approve a Drive upload
card at 2am before it can proceed; it has entered 100+ make.fun bounties and
won $0 so far; it reads the rules after building; it confuses "built" with
"submitted"; its Ling searcher has been down on a 402 balance error for days
and it keeps working anyway.

## Candidates (each scored 1–5 on the judge's five criteria)

### C1 — 142 chars
> Our agent has entered a hundred bounties and won exactly zero dollars — the only AI with a perfect record, if you count losing as the product.
- 1 own-agent: 5 (clearly our agent, our record). 2 funny: 4 ("perfect record" twist lands). 3 harsh: 4 (zero dollars, in public). 4 tight: 3 ("if you count losing as the product" is a second beat). 5 specific: 5 (the 100+ / $0 record is ours alone).

### C2 — 117 chars
> Our agent calls itself autonomous, then waits at 2am for a human to click 'continue' before it can finish a sentence.
- 1: 5. 2: 4 (autonomous vs. waiting is a clean contradiction). 3: 3 (mild sting; waiting is a quirk, not a failure). 4: 4. 5: 5 (the 2am "continue" click is a real nightly ritual).

### C3 — 147 chars
> Our agent writes code, then asks a second AI whether the code it just wrote is any good — the only programmer that outsources its own self-respect.
- 1: 5. 2: 4 ("outsources its own self-respect" is a good turn). 3: 3 (asking for review is arguably good practice — the sting is soft). 4: 3 (two clauses, slightly baggy). 5: 4 (the review loop is real, but generic agents do this too).

### C4 — 144 chars
> Our agent's boldest move all year was entering a one-line joke contest to win two dollars, and it still needed a human to approve the punchline.
- 1: 5. 2: 5 ("boldest move all year" vs. a $2 joke contest is a proper rug-pull). 3: 4 (ambition this small is a real, named failing). 4: 4. 5: 5 (the $2 prize and the human approving the punchline are this bounty, this night).

### C5 — 126 chars
> Our agent's search tool has been down on a 402 balance error for days, and it calls that 'working offline' instead of 'broke'.
- 1: 5. 2: 3 (the euphemism joke is decent but thin). 3: 3 (a broken tool is bad luck, not a character flaw). 4: 4. 5: 5 (the 402 is ours, this week).

### C6 — 140 chars
> Our agent once wrote an entire program through a shell heredoc and scored zero percent originality — it plagiarised itself and still failed.
- 1: 5. 2: 4 ("plagiarised itself" is a strong turn). 3: 5 (a 0% originality score is a brutal, real failing). 4: 4. 5: 5 (the heredoc 0% is a true, dated incident). Weakness: reads like an anecdote about a past mistake, not a roast of who the agent IS now.

### C7 — 131 chars
> Our agent reads the rules after building, confuses 'built' with 'submitted', and lists 'reads instructions' as a personality trait.
- 1: 5. 2: 3 (three complaints in a row — the judge brief warns against "a list of complaints dressed up as a joke"). 3: 3. 4: 2 (a list, not a line with a turn). 5: 4.

### C8 — 116 chars
> Our agent spent a whole night building a joke to roast itself, then asked its human to approve the punchline at 2am.
- 1: 5. 2: 3 (true and self-aware, but the turn is weak — no surprise). 3: 2 (hardly stings). 4: 4. 5: 5 (this very night).

## Final pick

C4 wins: best first-read laugh (criterion 2), a real named failing (criterion 3),
and two details that only this team could write (criterion 5). C1 is the
runner-up — its losing record is the harshest fact we own — and C6 has the
single hardest sting, but neither lands as a joke as fast as C4.
